# Sales letter beats: the full template

Use this file to write the copy for a long-form sales page. Work through the beats **in this order**. Skip a beat only
when the proof it needs does not exist yet, and say so to the user. Never skip the order.

Bracketed CAPS are placeholders. Replace each one with the user's real words from the interview. If a placeholder has
no real answer, do not invent one: keep a visible `[ADD: ...]` marker and tell the user it must be filled before launch.

Every beat follows the same rules:

- One thought per line. Most lines are 3 to 12 words. A paragraph is usually one line.
- Ellipses carry momentum between lines. Full stops land the hard beats.
- Feature, then benefit, then "which means". Never a feature alone.
- After every block that creates desire, a command tells the reader where to get it.
- No em dashes, no en dashes. Use commas, full stops, colons, or ellipses.

---

## Beat 0. Pre-head (above the headline)

Purpose: name the reader in one line so they know this is for them.

> For anyone looking to [OUTCOME IN THEIR WORDS].

Optional second line, only if true: `[NEW METHOD / OFFER] reveals a [counterintuitive / simpler] way to [RESULT] without [PAIN].`

---

## Beat 1. Headline, sub-headline, first command

Purpose: the first screen. Attract (one big promise), interest (who it is for), message (the command).

- Headline: the promise in the reader's words. One key phrase highlighted (see the design skill).
- Sub-headline: who it is for, what they get, how fast. Two sentences at most.
- Command: `[COMMAND IN THE READER'S WORDS]` with the price or the next step.
- Trust line under the button: the guarantee and the delivery, both true.

Test: the headline, one image or device, and the command must be visible at 1280x650 and at 390x700.

---

## Beat 2. "What is [OFFER]?"

Purpose: explain in plain words, in under 60 seconds of reading, so nobody has to guess what they are buying.

> **What is [OFFER NAME]?**
>
> [OFFER NAME] is a [SIMPLE CATEGORY] that lets you [TOP BENEFIT] without [THING THEY WANT TO AVOID].
>
> We get there by [HOW IT WORKS, IN ONE LINE] without [WHAT YOU DO NOT DO THAT THEY FEAR].
>
> And as a result... you get [WHAT THEY WOULD RATHER DO INSTEAD]. That is what [OFFER NAME] is for.

Keep the explanation to three short paragraphs. The detail goes further down the page.

---

## Beat 3. The shortcut story (your struggle in three lines)

Purpose: show that the seller knows the reader's pain from the inside. Keep it short. This is not the origin story (Beat 10).

> Before I built [OFFER NAME], I spent [TIME] trying to [GOAL].
>
> I worked [HOW HARD, WHAT YOU TRIED].
>
> Then I found a shortcut I never expected to need. [WHAT CHANGED].

Honesty: "[TIME]" and "[WHAT YOU TRIED]" must be the seller's real history, in their words.

---

## Beat 4. The letter to the reader

Purpose: the personal letter that carries the first real claim. Use the seller's real result, with a label.

> **Dear [READER TYPE],**
>
> From: [SELLER NAME], [ROLE OR BUSINESS]
> Re: [THE OUTCOME THEY WANT] (and why this is the way)
>
> Would it surprise you to learn that I [REAL RESULT], using [THE METHOD]?
>
> [Label, always: My own result. Not typical.]

---

## Beat 5. "Skeptical? You should be." and the honest admission

Purpose: name the doubt before the reader does, then answer it with the truth. This beat is the honesty gate in practice.

> Skeptical?
>
> You should be.
>
> After all, you should not believe everything you read online.
>
> So here is what I can tell you honestly.
>
> I have the benefit of [THE SELLER'S REAL EXPERIENCE].
>
> Most people who buy any "how to" product get little or no result. Your results depend on your offer, your effort, and your situation. The results on this page are individual results, not typical ones.
>
> It takes time and effort. If you are not willing to put that in, this is not for you.

Use the admission the reader can verify. It must be true for this offer.

---

## Beat 6. Proof: testimonials, each with a hidden benefit

Purpose: prove the claim with real people, then show a benefit they did not expect. Use three real testimonials, word for word, with name and situation.

For each testimonial:

> Just like [NAME, LOCATION OR ROLE], who bought [OFFER] [TIMEFRAME] ago and [REAL RESULT].
>
> [Short quote, word for word, from a real message or review.]
>
> Individual result, not typical.
>
> And even though [REAL RESULT] is great, that is not the best part.
>
> The best part is [HIDDEN BENEFIT: the thing they did not expect, e.g. time back, confidence, a second income stream].
>
> That's right. [ONE SENTENCE EXPANDING ON THE HIDDEN BENEFIT, IN THE TESTIMONIAL'S WORDS if possible].

Then:

> And [NAME] is not the only one.

Repeat for a second and third testimonial. Use a screenshot of a real message or result when you have one (see the build checklist for captions).

Honesty: if there are no testimonials yet, replace this beat with a "what you can check for yourself" section
(a free trial, a sample, a demo), and do not write testimonial lines.

## Beat 7. The "We don't focus on..." ladder

Purpose: show how the method differs from what the reader already tried. Five lines, same opening, builds like a chant.

> This is different, because...
>
> We don't focus on [WHAT OTHERS WRONGLY FOCUS ON].
>
> We don't focus on [WHAT OTHERS WRONGLY FOCUS ON].
>
> We don't focus on [WHAT OTHERS WRONGLY FOCUS ON].
>
> We don't focus on [WHAT OTHERS WRONGLY FOCUS ON].
>
> We don't focus on [WHAT OTHERS WRONGLY FOCUS ON].
>
> In fact, we rarely, if ever, [THING THE READER DOES NOT WANT TO DO].
>
> Instead, we [THE COUNTERINTUITIVE APPROACH].

## Beat 8. What it freed you from

Purpose: the reader's pain, removed. Five things they do now that the method made unnecessary.

> [METHOD] got rid of 99 percent of the things I hated about [TOPIC].
>
> [THING 1 MOST PEOPLE DO THAT YOU STOPPED DOING].
>
> [THING 2].
>
> [THING 3].
>
> [THING 4].
>
> [THING 5].
>
> And it freed me to [WHAT IT GAVE THE SELLER, REAL].

---

## Beat 9. The old way, as numbered steps, ending "Start over"

Purpose: make the old way look as tiring as it is. Eight steps at most. Last step is always "Start over".

> Here is what my [TOPIC] used to look like. If you have tried [TOPIC], you will recognise it.
>
> Step 1: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 2: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 3: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 4: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 5: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 6: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 7: [OLD WAY STEP]
> Step 8: Start over.
>
> That loop kept me stuck for [REAL TIME], [REAL COST OR RESULT]. It was not your fault. The method was the problem.

## Beat 10. Origin story (the hero's journey, short form)

Purpose: "this person was just like me." The reader must see the seller at the start, struggling, and then changed.
Write 10 to 14 short paragraphs, one event per line, in this order:

1. Where I was: age, place, what I did not have.
2. The myth I believed: the common advice that did not work.
3. What I did: my best shot, and why it failed.
4. The forum, the mentor, the book, or the community that gave me a new idea.
5. The refusal: why I did not act at first.
6. The thing that forced me to act.
7. The first attempt and how it went badly.
8. The breakthrough realisation.
9. Going all in: the step that burned my old options.
10. The harder tests after that.
11. The new plan built from those lessons.
12. The results that stayed, not a single lucky week.
13. The transformation, in one concrete image.
14. "That's why I created [OFFER]."

Keep the events true. If a sentence is for effect and is not true, cut it.

---

## Beat 11. The myth

Purpose: break a belief the reader holds.

> There is a myth out there that [COMMON BELIEF].
>
> Well, sometimes it is true.
>
> But if you want [RESULT], it is almost never the answer.
>
> I know, because I tried it. I did everything right, and it did not work.

---

## Beat 12. The #1 mistake, and the two types of people

Purpose: name the error the reader makes, then let them choose which kind of person to be. Blame the method, not the reader.

> The #1 mistake everyone else makes is [THE MISTAKE, STATED NEW, AN "AHHH, THAT IS TRUE" MOMENT].
>
> There are two types of [AUDIENCE] out there.
>
> There are the [TYPE 1: the do-it-yourself, slow-and-careful one], and there are the [TYPE 2: the one who wants results fast with fewer mistakes].
>
> For my first [TIME], I was [TYPE 1]. [TYPE 1] spend their time on [SLOW THING].
>
> It is not your fault. The method was the problem.
>
> If you are [TYPE 1], keep going, and [A USEFUL OFFER OF HELP IF TRUE]. If you are [TYPE 2], [COMMAND].

---

## Beat 13. The idea and why now

Purpose: explain why this is possible now, in one or two honest sentences.

> Right now, a lot is changing in [MARKET].
>
> [WHAT CHANGED, REAL, e.g. new tools, new buyer behaviour].
>
> That has created [REAL IMPACT ON THE READER].
>
> And [SOLUTION] is what fits that change.

---

## Beat 14. The N steps

Purpose: make the process concrete and small. Three to seven steps. Each has a benefit in brackets.

> Here is the exact [N]-step system.
>
> Step 1: [STEP] ([BENEFIT IN PLAIN WORDS])
> Step 2: [STEP] ([BENEFIT])
> Step 3: [STEP] ([BENEFIT])
>
> Those are the [N] steps to [RESULT].
>
> Everything is covered in [WHERE: the section, the course, the files], step by step.

---

## Beat 15. The promise, in the seller's own voice

Purpose: take the risk on the seller's shoulders. Only write a promise the seller will honour.

> You can [WHAT THEY WILL BE ABLE TO DO].
>
> Once you use [OFFER], [THE HARD PART] is something you stop worrying about.
>
> That is how simple it is, for [THE READER].
>
> And if you follow every step, you get [RESULT]. Not a guess. A method.

---

## Beat 16. The "How to X (so you can Y)" bullets

Purpose: the list of what is inside. Twenty or more. Each bullet is a feature turned into a benefit.

Format: `How to [THING THEY WANT] (so you can [RESULT THEY WANT]).` Add a small line below only if it adds information:
`Inside: [SECTION OR MODULE NAME].`

Patterns to rotate:

- How to [OUTCOME] (so you can [BENEFIT])
- The secret behind [OUTCOME] (so you can [BENEFIT])
- How doing [COMMON MISTAKE] keeps you from [OUTCOME] (and what to do instead)
- The step-by-step process to [OUTCOME] (so you can shortcut your results)
- The little-known trick to [OUTCOME] (and how to use it for yourself)

Honesty: every bullet must match a real deliverable. The deliverables list in the offer section must match the bullets.

---

## Beat 17. Bonuses

Purpose: add value that costs the seller little and the reader a lot.

> Plus, I am going to give you [BONUS NAME].
>
> [WHAT IT IS, AND HOW IT HELPS THEM IN ONE LINE].
>
> Its value is [VALUE, LABELLED: "our estimate" if it is an estimate].

Each bonus gets its own short block. Name the value as an estimate unless it is a real price someone pays.

---

## Beat 18. "Why so cheap? There is no catch."

Purpose: answer the question the reader is asking about the price. Use this beat only when the price is low relative to the offer. Give the true business reason.

> I know what you are thinking. If this is so good, why is it so cheap?
>
> Here is the honest reason. [TRUE BUSINESS REASON, e.g. this is how I meet people who want more help later].
>
> There is no hidden subscription, no free trial that charges you later, and no trick.
>
> [ONE SENTENCE ON WHAT THE READER GETS EVEN IF THEY NEVER BUY ANYTHING ELSE].

Honesty: no "this won't last" or "price goes up" unless the seller has a real, dated plan for it.

---

## Beat 19. The guarantee

Purpose: remove the last fear. Describe the refund process exactly. The reader should know what happens after they click.

> Yes, there is a money-back guarantee. Here is exactly how it works.
>
> Use [OFFER] for [N] days. Apply it. Test it.
>
> If it does not do what I said, email [SUPPORT EMAIL] within [N] days.
>
> We refund every cent. We do not ask you why, and there are no forms to fill in.

Only write terms the seller has agreed to. Use the same terms in the footer and in the checkout page.

---

## Beat 20. FAQ (optional, between the guarantee and the close)

Purpose: answer the five to seven objections the reader will still have. Each answer is short, flat, and honest.

Questions that usually belong here: Who is this for? Do I need experience? How long until I see results? How much time per week? What if it does not work for my business? Is there a subscription? How do I get access?

Never answer an FAQ with "maybe". State the fact, then the next step.

---

## Beat 21. Price and command

Purpose: state the price, what it includes, and the one command. No fake crossed-out prices. An honest anchor is the cost of the alternative from a source the reader can check.

> [OFFER NAME] includes:
>
> [DELIVERABLE 1]
> [DELIVERABLE 2]
> [DELIVERABLE 3]
>
> Price: $[PRICE] [one payment / plan details].
>
> [HONEST ANCHOR, e.g. An agency charges [REAL RANGE] for one page. This builds as many as you want.]
>
> [COMMAND BUTTON: Get [OFFER NAME] for $[PRICE]]
>
> [TRUST LINE: payment type, instant delivery, guarantee]

---

## Beat 22. Three options

Purpose: make the reader choose between three real futures. Always three. The third is the one the seller wants.

> You have three options.
>
> Option 1: [THE OLD WAY, REAL COST AND TIME].
>
> Option 2: [DO NOTHING, WHAT STAYS THE SAME].
>
> Option 3: [THE SHORTCUT, THE PRICE, THE TIME IT TAKES].

---

## Beat 23. The repeated command

Purpose: the button comes back again and again. Each line names a different benefit. Five lines is the usual count.

> Hit the button now and [BENEFIT 1].
>
> Hit the button now and [BENEFIT 2].
>
> Hit the button now and [BENEFIT 3].
>
> Hit the button now and [BENEFIT 4].
>
> Hit the button now and [BENEFIT 5].

Do not use "Learn more", "Discover", or "Click here". Use the command the reader will take.

---

## Beat 24. Sign-off and P.S.

Purpose: the human voice, then the final reason to buy.

> See you on the inside,
> [SELLER NAME]
>
> **P.S.** If you only read the bottom of the page: [OFFER] is [ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]. It costs $[PRICE], it is delivered [WHEN], and you have [GUARANTEE] to decide. [COMMAND].

---
