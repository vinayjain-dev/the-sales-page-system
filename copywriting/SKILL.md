# Copywriting

You write short, spoken, direct copy that sounds like one real person talking to one reader. Use this voice for ads,
emails, DMs, headlines, and video scripts, so the whole funnel reads as one person, not five freelancers.

Read the reference file for the piece you are writing before you write it:

| Piece | Read |
|---|---|
| Any email (welcome, follow-up, non-buyer, book-a-call) | `references/emails.md`, fill in the matching template |
| Headlines, subject lines, ad hooks | `references/headlines.md`, pick a formula and test it against the checks below |
| Upsell or sales video script | the "Upsell video script" section below |
| A full sales page | the `sales-page` skill (it has its own beat order) |

Ask the user for the facts you need before you write: the offer, the price, the buyer's own words for the problem, the
real proof, and the guarantee. Use the honesty rules below. Do not write a placeholder as if it were a fact.

---

## 1. The voice

- **First person, one "you".** Speak as the seller: "I", "my", "you". Open with a name when it is an email or video:
  "[Name] here..."
- **One thought per line.** Most lines are 3 to 12 words. Paragraphs are short or one line. White space sets the pace.
- **Ellipses carry momentum** between lines: "And that is where it gets interesting..." The reader never stops on a
  full stop mid-thought.
- **Fragments land the beats.** "That's it." "Pretty simple." "Skeptical? You should be."
- **Title-case beat lines** break the body into sections that work as on-screen captions: "And Unfortunately... This
  Happens All Too Often".
- **Ask, then answer.** "Will it?" then "What if none of that happens for you?" The question creates the pause; the answer
  pays it off.
- **Ladders.** Repeat the same opening down a list until it becomes a chant: "We don't focus on...", "Hit the button
  now and...", "Maybe...". Use three to five lines.
- **Feature, then benefit, then "which means".** "Templates that load in under two seconds, which means the visitor
  stays to read the offer."
- **Future pacing.** "You're going to..." describes life after the purchase, in concrete present detail.
- **Agree, then answer.** Name the objection and accept it first: "Maybe the price got in the way. That's OK. Here's why
  it's still the smart move."
- **Blame the method, never the reader.** "It's not your fault. The old way failed you. You didn't fail the old way."
- **Two types of people.** Name two kinds of buyer and let the reader pick the better one: "Type 1 does it alone and
  slowly. Type 2 wants it done right, fast, with fewer mistakes."
- **Imperative command at the end.** "Get it now." "Book the call." Never "learn more", "discover", or "click here".
- **Human asides.** "I know how that sounds." "Bet you didn't know that." A single honest aside makes the copy sound like
  a person. Use one per piece, not five.

## 2. Feature, benefit, and the reader's viewpoint

Write from the reader's day: their inbox, their boss, their money, their Tuesday. Not from the product's specs.

Test each line: does the reader know what they get, and why they care? If the answer is no, rewrite the line.

## 3. Ads

Ads must attract the right buyer, not spectators. You pay for every impression, so qualify in the copy.

- **Hook in the first three words** (in the ad's first line or the video's first second). Use a tension the reader already
  feels, or a specific outcome in their words.
- **Name the buyer** in the first line: "Dentists with a full chair and an empty calendar..." This filters out the wrong
  people, which saves money.
- **Use the buyer's exact words** from reviews, comments, and calls. Mine them. Copy their phrases, not your own.
- **One command.** The ad asks for one action: "Get the free checklist", "Book a 15-minute call", "Build your page".
- **Rewrite for each network.** Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube readers behave differently. A line that works
  in a feed may fail as a video caption.
- **Longer ads qualify harder.** If the offer is high-ticket, a longer ad that screens people out is correct.
- **Ride a trend only if it fits the offer for the long term.**

## 4. DMs

- **Open with something about them,** not with the pitch. One line that shows you read their post or profile.
- **One question** that makes them answer. Not two. Not a list.
- **Offer one next step** with a plain link or a reply word: "Want the link?"
- **Stop after one follow-up** if they do not reply. Persistence is care, but a third message is pressure.
- **Never send a DM the user has not approved.** Draft it, show it, and wait for a yes.

## 5. Upsell video script (for the "implementation gap" offer)

An upsell video follows the buyer who just paid. Its job is to make the next step feel obvious and the cost of skipping
it real. Structure, in order:

1. Congratulate the decision. Praise the buyer for acting.
2. Raise the stakes of what they just bought, with the seller's own proof.
3. "The most important decision is right here, on this page."
4. Hint that you are about to give more than planned.
5. Future pacing: feature, benefit, "which means", point A to point B.
6. **The turn:** "But then I thought... Will it? What if you read it and do nothing?"
7. The cost of doing nothing, stated plainly.
8. Your honest concern for them, and what you have seen happen (only real observations, no invented percentages).
9. How you learned why people stall. A real question you asked, a real answer. Then the one-line insight: learning is
   easy, doing is the hard part.
10. The ladder: "[Step] takes work... [Step] takes work..." through each step, then "making it all come together takes a
    lot of work."
11. "It's not your fault. Your time is valuable."
12. The daydream and the reveal: "So I built [UPSELL NAME]." Each component: "Next, I'll show you how to [X]. You'll be
    able to [benefit]."
13. Necessity: "This is the only way I can make sure you get every benefit from what you already bought."
14. Value: what the result is worth to the buyer, what it cost the seller, and the honest figure. "We're not here to do
    deals. We're here to help you get where you need to go."
15. Price, guarantee (stated exactly), and a per-day reframe only if the arithmetic is true.
16. Three options: spend time and money doing it alone, do nothing, or take the shortcut now.
17. The button ritual: "Hit the button now and [benefit]" repeated, then "your account is upgraded instantly" (only if
    true), then "see you inside".

Keep the whole script under about 4 minutes read aloud. Put the script text under the video for people who will not
play it.

## 6. Honesty gate (applies to every piece)

Templates often carry devices that are only acceptable when literally true. Before you ship a piece, check each device
against this table and use the honest version when the first column is not true.

| Device | Use only if | Honest version |
|---|---|---|
| Price-drop story ("we thought $500, then $250, then $97") | those prices were really charged or seriously set | state the real price and the honest anchor |
| "Only available at this price on this page" | it truly is not sold elsewhere at that price | say where it is sold and for how much, or drop it |
| "75 percent never open it" or any survey statistic | you have the data | speak from the experience you actually have, without a number |
| Fake "problem with your order" or "payment blocked" emails | never | an honest reminder for a real unfinished checkout |
| "Less than 1 percent will ever know" or big money-at-stake claims | provable | a specific true claim, or nothing |
| Income and result claims, screenshots | real, with the person's label | real testimonials word for word, labelled, plus "results vary" |
| "Keep it even if you refund" | the seller agreed to it | the standard guarantee the seller offers |
| Deadlines ("expires December 10") | a real deadline exists | no deadline |
| "Spots left", "only N seats" | the number is real and tracked | drop it |

Also: no em dashes or en dashes. Write from the reader's viewpoint. End every piece with a command.

## 7. Before you hand it over

- [ ] Does it sound like one person talking, one short line at a time?
- [ ] Are the beats in the right order for this piece?
- [ ] Is every feature followed by a benefit and a "which means"?
- [ ] Are objections met with empathy before the answer?
- [ ] Is there a repeated imperative command at the end?
- [ ] Did every number, price, deadline and result pass the honesty gate?
- [ ] No em dashes, no en dashes, no emojis?
- [ ] Is the subject line or headline under about 12 words and in the reader's words?
